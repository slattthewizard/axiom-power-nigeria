A 200 kVA standby set at a private hospital on the Lagos mainland ran eleven months without a single fault code, then lost three injectors and a high pressure fuel pump inside the same fortnight. The repair came to N6.4m, about $4,100, plus four days on hired plant. The tanker that caused it had made its last delivery nine weeks earlier.

Contaminated diesel generator damage almost never announces itself on the day the bad fuel arrives. It accumulates in the bottom of a tank, migrates through a filter that nobody is monitoring, and presents months later as an expensive mechanical failure that gets written up as bad luck or a bad batch of parts. By then the evidence has been burnt.

This article sets out where the contamination comes from, what it does inside the injection system, the checks you can run without a laboratory, and what the failures cost in naira.

## How Diesel Gets Contaminated Between the Depot and Your Day Tank

Fuel that leaves a licensed depot on specification can still reach your injectors out of specification. There are four routes, and most sites are exposed to at least three.

Water is the first and the most common. It arrives dissolved in the fuel, condenses inside a half empty tank as temperature swings between day and night, and enters through loose fill caps, failed gauges and rainwater on a poorly graded tank pad. In the humid coastal air of Lagos, Port Harcourt and Warri, a half full tank makes its own water without help from a supplier.

Particulate is the second. Rust and scale from the inside of an ageing steel tank, sand and harmattan dust drawn in through an unfiltered tank vent, and welding slag or grit left behind at installation all end up in suspension. Fine particulate is the material that scores pump elements and cuts injector seats.

Microbial growth is the third, and it is misunderstood. What operators call diesel bug lives at the interface between water and fuel, not in the fuel itself, so removing the free water removes the habitat. Leave 30 mm of water in a warm tank for three months and it will produce a dark slime that blinds filters and releases biomass in slugs whenever the tank is disturbed.

Adulteration and degradation make up the fourth. Product blended down the supply chain, and simple oxidation of fuel held too long, both change how the fuel burns and where it deposits. Diesel held beyond six to twelve months without a stabiliser forms gums that lacquer injector needles.

## What Contaminated Diesel Generator Damage Looks Like Inside the Engine

The injection system is the part that pays. Diesel is not just the fuel in a modern engine, it is also the lubricant and coolant for the high pressure pump and the injector internals, and it is metered through clearances measured in single microns.

Water destroys that in three separate ways. It displaces the fuel film in the pump and allows metal to metal contact, it flashes to steam at the injector tip and erodes the nozzle by cavitation, and it corrodes the ground surfaces of plungers and needles into a pitted finish that no longer seals. A common rail system operating at 1,600 to 2,500 bar has no tolerance for any of that.

Particulate works faster and more visibly. Abrasive wear opens the clearance between plunger and barrel, delivery pressure falls, the injector begins to dribble instead of atomising, and combustion goes ragged. The visible symptoms are black smoke, hard starting, hunting under load and a drop in available output, and the wider diagnostic path is covered in our note on [generator low output causes](/blog/generator-low-output-causes/).

Microbial contamination attacks differently. Biomass blinds filters and starves the engine, which shows up as a set that runs cleanly at light load and shuts down on low fuel pressure the moment it is asked for full output. The organisms also produce organic acids that pit the tank floor and the fuel gallery.

There is a secondary chain of damage that costs more than the injectors. Poor atomisation means incomplete combustion, which on a lightly loaded set accelerates wet stacking, bore glazing and turbocharger fouling, a mechanism set out in our article on [diesel generator wet stacking](/blog/diesel-generator-wet-stacking/). Unburnt fuel also dilutes the lube oil, which is why fuel contamination often shows in an oil sample first.

By the time the engine misbehaves you are already paying for parts. The window in which the problem was cheap closed months earlier, at the filter and the dip stick.

## The Fuel Quality Checks Every Plant Should Run

Most of what matters can be established on site by a competent technician with simple equipment. Laboratory work is for confirming a suspicion and for holding a supplier to account, not for routine surveillance.

The single most useful indicator costs nothing. Track filter life in running hours and write it down. A primary filter that used to last 500 hours and now lasts 120 is telling you the tank is dirty, and it is telling you long before the engine does.

| Check | What it detects | Frequency | Indicative cost (NGN) | Act when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tank dip with water finding paste, then drain the sump | Free water and settled sludge | Weekly | 15,000 per tube | Any colour change, or cloudy dark drawings |
| Filter differential pressure and element life log | Particulate and biomass loading | Every service | 30,000 for a gauge kit | Element life falls by a third or more |
| Clear jar sample from tank bottom and day tank | Water haze, sediment, dark slime | Monthly | Labour only | Sample is not bright and clear |
| Microbial dip slide or ATP test | Bacteria and fungi at the water interface | Quarterly | 25,000 to 60,000 per test | Any moderate or heavy growth reading |
| Sealed sample retained from each tanker | Supplier accountability, adulteration | Every delivery | Cost of a sample bottle | Density, colour or smell varies from the norm |
| Full laboratory fuel analysis | Water and sediment, density, flash point, cleanliness code | Annually, or in dispute | 120,000 to 280,000 | Any result outside the fuel specification |

The clear jar test is the most underrated tool on a Nigerian plant. Draw a sample from the lowest point of the tank into a clean glass jar, stand it for fifteen minutes and hold it against a white background. Clean diesel is bright and clear. Haze means suspended water, a layer at the bottom means free water, and dark stringy material at the interface means a biological problem rather than a housekeeping one.

The retained delivery sample changes commercial behaviour. When a haulier knows a sealed and labelled sample is taken from every load and kept for ninety days, the quality of what arrives improves. Downstream petroleum product supply is regulated by the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority at [nmdpra.gov.ng](https://www.nmdpra.gov.ng/). Cleanliness is expressed under the ISO 4406 coding system, published by [iso.org](https://www.iso.org/), and many common rail engine makers specify a code in the region of 18/16/13 or cleaner at the injector. Bulk fuel reaching a typical Nigerian industrial tank is routinely several codes dirtier, which is why filtration matters more here than the engine manual assumes.

## What Diesel Contamination Damage Costs: Two Field Cases

Numbers argue better than principles in a budget meeting, so here are two, both anonymised.

A cassava processing plant in Delta State ran two 350 kVA sets from a 20,000 litre bunded steel tank installed without a sump or a drain valve. Water bottoms had accumulated for four years with no way to remove them. Nobody noticed until primary filter life fell from roughly 400 hours to 90 hours over one rainy season, and the response was to buy more filters.

The failure arrived in the dry season. One set lost its high pressure pump and a full set of six injectors, and the strip found pitted plunger surfaces consistent with water and abrasive wear rather than age. Parts and labour came to N9.7m, about $6,300, with an eight week lead time on the pump because it was not a stocked item. The plant hired 400 kVA of standby at N2.1m a month for two months and lost eleven production shifts it valued at N14.3m, taking total exposure to roughly N28m, close to $18,000.

Prevention would have cost a fraction of that. A sump and drain valve retrofit at N850k, a two stage coalescing filtration and polishing loop at N2.9m, and a weekly dip and drain routine that takes one technician ten minutes. The second case is smaller and more typical. A 60-bed clinic in Kaduna ran a 100 kVA set on a 5,000 litre tank topped up in 800 litre drums rather than by tanker. Drum transfer with a hand pump and an open funnel is an efficient way to introduce dust and water, and the tank was never more than a third full, so it breathed heavily every night.

The set began shutting down on low fuel pressure during theatre hours. The clinic changed filters four times in six weeks, replaced the lift pump, then called for help. A dip slide returned heavy fungal growth and the jar sample showed 40 mm of water with a black interface layer. Corrective work was a full drain and mechanical clean at N620k, shock and maintenance biocide dosing at N310k, a filtration upgrade at N1.4m and two damaged injectors at N1.9m, totalling N4.23m or about $2,700. The N780k already spent on repeat filters and a pump had achieved nothing, because nobody had tested the fuel.

Both sites made the same mistake. They treated a fuel problem as a parts problem.

## Filtration, Polishing and Tank Housekeeping That Actually Works

The engineering answer has three layers, and they are not interchangeable.

Housekeeping comes first because it is nearly free. Keep the bulk tank as full as practicable to cut the air volume that condenses water, fit and use a drain valve at the true low point, dip and drain weekly, fit a desiccant or filtered breather, rotate stock so no fuel sits beyond about six months, and grade the tank pad so rainwater runs away from the fill point.

Filtration comes second. A proper arrangement has a water coalescing separator upstream, a particulate stage sized to the engine maker's requirement, and the engine mounted filters left as a last line rather than the primary defence. Fit a differential pressure gauge and a water in fuel sensor alarmed to the panel, because a filter that is quietly blinding is invisible until the set trips.

Polishing comes third, and it is remedial rather than a substitute for the other two. A kidney loop rig circulates the tank contents through coalescing and particulate stages on a timer, suppressing water and biomass between deliveries. It will not rescue a tank that already has a sludge layer and a corroded floor. That needs draining, mechanical cleaning and internal inspection, which means confined space entry, gas testing and a qualified crew.

Biocide kills the organisms without removing the dead biomass, so a shock dose in a heavily infected tank releases a wave of debris that blocks every filter downstream within hours. Dose after cleaning, at the stated concentration, and plan for filter changes immediately afterwards.

Fuel testing sits alongside lube oil analysis rather than replacing it, because fuel dilution and soot loading are often the earliest evidence that combustion has degraded. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if contaminated diesel is damaging my generator?

The earliest reliable signal is filter life. If elements are blinding faster than they used to, the fuel is carrying water, particulate or biomass and the injection system is already exposed. Confirm with a clear jar sample from the tank bottom and a microbial dip slide before replacing any parts, because new injectors in a dirty tank only reset the clock.

### Can water in a diesel tank be removed without draining the whole tank?

Often yes, if it is still free water at the low point and the tank has a sump and drain valve. Weekly draining plus a coalescing filtration or polishing loop will manage it. Once there is a sludge layer, microbial growth or corrosion of the floor, a full drain, mechanical clean and internal inspection is the only honest answer, and that needs confined space procedures and a qualified crew.

### What does injector and fuel pump damage from bad diesel cost in Nigeria?

For a set in the 100 to 350 kVA range, expect roughly N1.5m to N5.5m for a full set of injectors and N3.5m to N11m for a high pressure pump, depending on engine make and whether the part is stocked locally. Lead times on non-stocked injection components commonly run six to twelve weeks, and hired standby plus lost production over that period usually exceeds the parts bill. Prevention typically costs N1m to N4m as a one off.

### How often should a Nigerian plant test its diesel fuel?

Dip and drain weekly, log filter differential pressure and element life at every service, draw a clear jar sample monthly and run a microbial dip slide quarterly. Send a sample for full laboratory analysis annually, or immediately if you are in dispute with a supplier or have had an unexplained injection failure. Sites with high humidity, low fuel turnover or drum based transfer should tighten those intervals, because the exposure is site specific.
