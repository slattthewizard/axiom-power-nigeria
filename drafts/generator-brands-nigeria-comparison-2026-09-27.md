# Mikano, Perkins, Caterpillar, Cummins, FG Wilson or Jubaili: Comparing Generator Brands in Nigeria

Every procurement officer who searches for a Mikano generator price in Nigeria, or runs the same search against Perkins, Caterpillar, Cummins, FG Wilson or Jubaili, is trying to answer a harder question than a number on a quote. The name on the canopy tells you very little on its own. What matters for an industrial set that has to run for thousands of hours a year in Nigerian heat and dust is the engine underneath the badge, where the alternator and controller come from, and how quickly a part reaches your site when something fails.

This is a comparison for maintenance and procurement people, not a price list. Our price list is not published, and any figure a dealer quotes today will move with the exchange rate between now and delivery, so it has to be requested fresh rather than copied from an old brochure. What we can compare, and what should carry more weight in a decision than the badge, is engine origin, local assembly, alternator and controller choice, parts and dealer support, and how each brand's typical service intervals line up with the way your plant actually runs.

## Engine origin: what actually powers the set

Several of the names buyers compare are not competing generator makers so much as different points in the same supply chain, and knowing which is which changes how you read a quote.

Perkins is a UK engine manufacturer, not a stand-alone genset brand sold directly to end users in Nigeria. Perkins has been a subsidiary of Caterpillar Inc since 1998. When someone searches for a "Perkins generator price in Nigeria" they are almost always looking at a complete set assembled by someone else around a Perkins engine, most commonly Mikano or Jubaili Bros. That distinction matters at the point of sale: the engine warranty and parts network run through Perkins, but the alternator, controller, canopy, base tank and after-sales service are the assembler's responsibility, and the two do not always match in quality.

Mikano is Nigeria's largest local assembler of power generating sets. It runs an assembly line in Nigeria built around Perkins (UK) and MTU Onsite Energy (Germany) engines paired with Stamford or Mecc Alte alternators, across a wide kVA range from small commercial sets up to units sized for heavy industrial and utility load. Mikano is also an authorised Perkins dealer, which is one reason its Perkins-engined sets carry a recognisable local parts and warranty chain.

FG Wilson began in Belfast in 1966 and has been owned by Caterpillar since 1998 to 1999. Its gensets are built around Perkins engines under a long-standing exclusive supply arrangement, and within the Caterpillar group FG Wilson sits as the utility-tier brand, positioned below the Cat-branded range, which is Caterpillar's premium tier using its own engines and alternators. A buyer comparing a Cat-branded set against an FG Wilson set of similar output is, in effect, comparing two brands from the same parent company at different market positions rather than two independent manufacturers.

Cummins is a US engine and generator manufacturer that builds both its own engines and complete gensets under the Cummins name, sold through Cummins-authorised channels rather than a third-party assembler.

Jubaili Bros is a Lebanon-founded group with factories in Lebanon, Dubai and Nigeria. It is an authorised OEM of Perkins engines and its sets are primarily Perkins-powered, though some models are offered with a Cummins engine option instead. Its Nigerian assembly presence puts it in a similar local-build category to Mikano for parts of its range.

None of this tells you which set will suit your plant. It tells you what question to ask the dealer standing in front of you: which engine is actually fitted, where was the set assembled, and who carries the parts and warranty obligation if the engine and the badge on the canopy belong to different companies.

## Alternators and controllers: the part of the quote buyers skim past

The engine gets most of the attention in a brand comparison, but the alternator and the controller drive as much of your maintenance load. Stamford and Mecc Alte are the alternator names that turn up most often on Nigerian industrial sets, fitted by multiple assemblers rather than tied to one brand. The controller, commonly a Deep Sea Electronics or ComAp unit, governs starting, protection, alarms and, on larger installations, transfer switching and synchronising logic. Two sets with the same engine can behave very differently in service depending on which controller is fitted and how well it was configured at commissioning, because the controller is what actually reports the fault before it becomes a shutdown.

When you compare quotes across brands, ask each assembler to state the alternator make and frame size and the controller model in writing rather than accepting "industrial grade" as a description. A mismatch between alternator rating and engine output, or a controller that cannot log the alarm history you need for a maintenance record, causes more day-to-day friction than the engine badge does.

## Parts availability and lead time

Parts availability tends to follow assembly footprint. Brands that assemble or hold significant stock in Nigeria, such as Mikano and Jubaili Bros, generally move common wear parts (filters, belts, sensors) faster than a brand whose spares route through a single import channel with no local stockholding. That is a generalisation, not a guarantee: stock levels shift, and the only reliable check is to ask a prospective supplier for their actual lead time on the specific part numbers your engine model uses, in writing, before you commit. ## Dealer and service network

Coverage matters more once a set is installed than at the point of purchase, because that is when you need someone who can source the specific part number and who has technicians familiar with that controller's fault codes. A brand with wide geographic reach across Lagos, Port Harcourt, Abuja, Kaduna, Warri and Kano reduces the distance a part or a technician has to travel when a set trips at 2 a.m. We are not ranking individual dealers here, and a dealer's claimed response time is not something we can verify or promise on their behalf; ask any dealer for their actual coverage map and their documented escalation process rather than a verbal assurance.

## Service intervals and how they line up with your load

Manufacturer datasheets for a typical industrial diesel set in this class show routine service intervals in the region of 250 to 500 running hours for oil, filters and general checks, with more detailed work at annual or major-hour milestones, though the OEM manual for your specific engine and generation set is the document that governs, not a generic figure. What differs by brand in practice is less the interval itself and more whether the local dealer stocks the filter kit for your engine model and whether the controller logs run hours accurately enough for you to plan around. A set that is technically excellent but whose local dealer cannot supply a fuel filter within a reasonable lead time will cost you more downtime than a modestly specified set backed by a responsive parts chain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Mikano a generator manufacturer or an assembler?

Mikano assembles generating sets in Nigeria around imported engines, principally Perkins from the UK and MTU Onsite Energy from Germany, paired with Stamford or Mecc Alte alternators. It does not manufacture the engine itself, but its local assembly and authorised Perkins dealership give it a recognisable parts and warranty chain inside Nigeria.

### What is the difference between an FG Wilson and a Caterpillar generator?

Both are owned by Caterpillar, but they sit at different tiers of the same product family. FG Wilson uses Perkins engines and is positioned as the utility-tier option, while Cat-branded sets use Caterpillar's own engines and alternators as the premium tier, so comparing the two is closer to comparing trims within one manufacturer than comparing rival brands.

### Does Perkins sell complete generators in Nigeria?

Perkins is an engine manufacturer, not a genset seller in its own right. A generator advertised as Perkins-powered has been assembled by another company, commonly Mikano or Jubaili Bros in the Nigerian market, using a Perkins engine, and the assembler is who you deal with for the finished set, the controller and most of the after-sales service.

### Which generator brand has the best parts availability in Nigeria?

Availability tends to track local assembly and stockholding rather than any one brand name across the board, and it can change over time. Ask any prospective supplier for the actual lead time on the specific filters, sensors and controller parts your engine model uses, in writing, rather than relying on a general reputation for good stock.

### Should engine origin decide which generator brand to buy?

Engine origin is one factor among several, alongside alternator and controller specification, local parts and dealer support, and how the set's typical service interval fits your run hours. A well-supported set with a modest specification often outperforms a premium engine backed by a thin local parts chain, so the buying decision should weigh the whole support package rather than the engine badge alone.
