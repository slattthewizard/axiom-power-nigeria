# How to Choose an Engineering Services Company in Nigeria for Plant and Power Equipment

A bad hire on a turbine overhaul or a generator repair does not show up as a bad invoice. It shows up three months later as a repeat trip, a machine that trips on load, or a report you cannot hand to your insurer or your board. Choosing among the engineering companies in Nigeria that pitch for plant and power maintenance work is harder than comparing quotes, because the quote rarely tells you who will actually be on the tools, what they will check before they touch a live circuit, or whether the parts they fit are genuine.

This article sets out the criteria that matter for a maintenance manager, procurement officer or facility manager picking a contractor for turbines, generators, compressors, pumps or plant electrical work. It does not name or rank any company. The point is to give you a checklist you can run against any proposal that lands on your desk, whether it is for a single call-out or a multi-year maintenance contract.

## What the Work Actually Requires

Before comparing contractors, be clear about what the job needs. Turbine, generator and rotating equipment work spans several distinct competences: mechanical fitting and alignment, combustion and controls, electrical protection and switching, and increasingly, condition monitoring and data analysis. A contractor who is strong on diesel engine overhauls is not automatically qualified to work on a steam turbine's governor system or a plant's power factor correction panel.

Start by writing down the scope you actually need serviced: engine type, alternator make, control system (DSE, ComAp or similar), site conditions (dust, heat, salt air near the coast), and whether the work includes electrical isolation, pressurised systems or working at height. A contractor's proposal should map directly onto that scope, not a generic "we service all makes" line. If a proposal does not mention your specific equipment class, ask for a written statement of relevant experience with it.

## Registration and Qualification: What COREN Membership Actually Means

The Council for the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria (COREN) was established to regulate the education, training and practice of engineering in the country. A Registered Engineer under COREN's framework holds an accredited engineering qualification, has completed a minimum period of professional experience, and has passed the Council's professional practice examination before being entered on the register (COREN, coren.gov.ng).

That distinction matters when you are hiring for work that involves electrical protection settings, pressure vessel decisions or sign-off on structural or safety-critical repairs. Ask any contractor for the COREN registration status of the engineers who will actually supervise and sign off your job, not just the company's marketing material. A firm can genuinely employ registered engineers on some jobs and unregistered technicians on others: the paperwork should tell you who is assigned to yours. Be equally alert to vague claims. A contractor should be able to name the specific registration category and produce a certificate on request; if a proposal only says the company is "certified" or "licensed" without naming the body, treat that as a gap to close before you sign, not a detail to overlook.

## HSE Record and Documentation

For industrial power and rotating equipment, most of the serious injury risk sits in three places: electrical isolation, work on pressurised or rotating machinery, and lifting. A contractor's health and safety record is not a courtesy question, it is a direct predictor of how the job will run on your site.

Ask for:

- A written permit-to-work and lockout/tagout procedure, and evidence it is actually used, not just filed.
- Incident and near-miss records for the past two to three years, including how each was closed out.
- Method statements and risk assessments specific to your job, not a generic template with your company name pasted in.
- Confirmation of who carries insurance for site injury, third-party damage and professional indemnity, and at what level.

Where the job touches electrical isolation, insulation testing, transfer switching or pressurised systems, the work must be carried out by qualified personnel following a permit to work with proper lockout/tagout. A contractor who is casual about this on the phone will usually be casual about it on site.

## Tooling and Diagnostic Capability

A contractor's toolbox tells you more than its brochure. For rotating equipment and generator work, the baseline kit includes calibrated vibration analysis equipment, insulation resistance and polarisation index testers, thermal imaging, and oil sampling kits linked to a laboratory that reports against recognised limits rather than a guess. For turbines specifically, ask whether the contractor owns or can access borescope equipment for hot gas path and combustion inspection, and whether their instruments carry current calibration certificates.

Do not accept "we have all the equipment" as an answer. Ask for calibration certificate dates on the instruments that will be used on your job, and ask which tests will actually be run and reported, not just performed.

## OEM Data Access and the Spares Chain

Two failures repeat across Nigerian plants: repairs done against a generic manual rather than the machine's actual OEM documentation, and repairs delayed for weeks because a part had to be sourced without a clear supply chain. Ask a prospective contractor:

- Do they work from the OEM's manual and parts catalogue for your specific model, or from general experience with "similar" machines.
- How they source spares: through an authorised distributor, a broker, or informal import, and what that means for lead time and part authenticity.
- What happens when a critical part is not in stock. A contractor who has no answer beyond "we will source it" has not thought through your outage window.

Counterfeit filters, seals and electronic modules are a live risk in the Nigerian aftermarket. A contractor who cannot describe how they verify a part's authenticity before fitting it is passing that risk on to you.

## Reporting Standards You Should Expect

A repair or an overhaul that leaves you with no paper trail is a liability, not a saving. At minimum, expect a written report for every job that covers: findings before work started, work carried out, parts replaced with part numbers, test results (insulation resistance, vibration readings, alignment figures, load test data as applicable), and any outstanding recommendations. That report should exist whether the job was a routine service or an emergency repair.

For anything approaching an overhaul, ask to see a sample scope-of-work document from a past job (with client details redacted) before you sign. If a contractor cannot produce one, that is itself informative.

## Comparing Contract Structures

The way a contract is structured changes the contractor's incentives as much as the price does. The table below sets out the common structures for plant and power maintenance work in Nigeria and what each one suits.

| Structure | How it works | Best suited to | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call-out / time and materials | Billed per visit, labour and parts as used | Ad hoc repairs, low equipment count | No incentive to prevent recurrence; costs hard to forecast |
| Scheduled maintenance contract | Fixed scope of visits at set intervals (see /blog/generator-maintenance-contract/) | Sites with a small, known fleet | Scope must specify tasks per visit, not just "service" |
| Framework / call-off agreement | Pre-agreed rates and response terms, work called off as needed | Multi-site operators, fluctuating workload | Rates should be locked for a defined period against currency movement |
| Full outsourced O&M | Contractor takes ongoing responsibility for defined equipment | Larger IPPs, captive plants with limited in-house staff | KPIs and reporting cadence must be contractual, not verbal |

None of these structures removes the need for a scoped, written statement of work. A one-line contract that just says "maintenance services" leaves both sides guessing what was actually promised. For a worked example of what a proper scope of work should contain for a turbine job, see /blog/turbine-overhaul-scope-of-work/.

## Weighing Cost Against Total Risk

Price will always be part of the decision, but it should be the last filter, not the first. Rank shortlisted contractors against the criteria above, eliminate anyone who fails on registration, HSE or reporting, and only then compare pricing among the survivors. A cheaper quote from a contractor with no calibrated instruments or no written HSE procedure is not actually cheaper once you account for the downtime cost of a repeat failure or a rejected insurance claim following an incident.

If your plant does not yet have a documented maintenance contract in place, or you are reviewing one that has drifted into informal call-outs, a technical proposal scoped to your actual equipment and site conditions is the starting point. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is COREN registration a legal requirement for all engineering work in Nigeria?

COREN regulates the practice and title of engineering in Nigeria, and certain categories of work, particularly those requiring an engineer's professional sign-off, are expected to involve registered personnel. For maintenance and repair work generally, ask which specific individuals on a job are COREN-registered and in what category, rather than accepting a blanket claim about the company.

### Should I use one contractor for everything, or split turbine, generator and electrical work between specialists?

It depends on your fleet size and the contractor's actual depth across disciplines. A single contractor covering mechanical, electrical and controls can simplify coordination, but only if they can demonstrate real competence in each area rather than subcontracting quietly. For a smaller site with one or two equipment classes, a specialist is often the safer choice.

### How many years of experience should a contractor have before I trust them with an overhaul?

Years in business is a weak signal on its own. What matters is documented experience with your specific equipment class, the qualifications of the engineers actually assigned to your job, and a reporting standard you can review before committing. Ask for evidence tied to the machine type, not a general number of years.

### What is the biggest red flag when vetting a maintenance contractor?

Vagueness. A contractor who cannot name the specific tests they will run, cannot produce a calibration certificate for their instruments, or cannot explain how they source and verify spare parts is asking you to trust process you cannot check. Push for specifics before signing anything.

### Can a contract lock in pricing to protect against exchange rate movement?

Framework agreements can fix labour rates for a defined period, but imported parts and consumables priced in foreign currency will still move with the exchange rate. Any contract should state clearly which elements are fixed and which are subject to review, rather than leaving currency risk unaddressed until an invoice arrives.
