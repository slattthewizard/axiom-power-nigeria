# Gas Compressor Maintenance: Reciprocating and Centrifugal Machines in Nigerian Plants

A gas compressor that trips at 2 a.m. does not announce itself politely. It shows up as a lost shift, a flared stream, or a facility running on a single train while the other sits stripped down waiting for a gasket set. Gas compressor maintenance in Nigerian oil and gas facilities and gas-to-power plants has to deal with two very different machine families under one heading, and the failure modes, the monitoring, and the overhaul planning are not the same for both.

This guide covers reciprocating and centrifugal gas compressors separately: how each fails, what a maintenance team should be watching between overhauls, and how to plan the next major work scope before the machine forces the decision. It does not cover screw air compressors used for utility air, which behave differently and are covered separately in our guide to [screw compressor maintenance](/blog/screw-compressor-maintenance/).

Reciprocating and centrifugal machines share a maintenance philosophy even where the mechanics diverge: catch the early symptom, understand the cause before you touch the machine, and plan the overhaul around condition rather than a fixed calendar date alone.

## Reciprocating and Centrifugal Gas Compressors: Two Different Failure Patterns

Reciprocating compressors handle gas through a piston moving inside a cylinder, using suction and discharge valves that open and close on pressure differential rather than a timed mechanism. They cover a wide range of pressures and flows and tolerate composition swings reasonably well, which suits associated gas and low-pressure gathering duty. API 618 sets out the design and testing basis most vendors work to for reciprocating compressors in petroleum, chemical and gas service, and it is worth asking a supplier whether a given machine was built to that standard.

Centrifugal compressors move gas by accelerating it through a rotating impeller and converting velocity into pressure in a diffuser. They suit continuous, high-flow duty at fairly steady composition, which is why they turn up on gas injection, gas lift and pipeline booster services, and on the compressor trains feeding gas turbines in combined cycle plants. API 617 covers centrifugal and axial compressors in this service class.

The practical difference for a maintenance manager is where the failures cluster. Reciprocating machines wear through repeated mechanical contact: valves, packing, piston rings, crossheads. Centrifugal machines fail through aerodynamic instability, seal degradation and fouling on the gas path, with far fewer wearing parts but less forgiving failure modes when something does go wrong. A large share of Nigeria's gas compression duty, associated gas handling, gas lift and gas injection alike, sits in and around Port Harcourt and the wider Rivers State oil and gas corridor, which is where this equipment mix concentrates most heavily.

## Reciprocating Gas Compressor Failure Modes

The valves are usually the first thing to go, and the most frequent repair item on a reciprocating gas compressor. Valve plates and springs fatigue from repeated impact, and dirty or wet gas accelerates the wear. A worn valve shows up as reduced capacity, a rise in discharge temperature on that cylinder, and a distinct change in the valve's characteristic sound, which experienced operators learn to pick out.

Packing, the seals around the piston rod where it passes through the cylinder wall, wears from rod-to-packing friction and from any misalignment in the rod run. Packing leakage shows as gas at the distance piece vent, oil carryover if the packing is lubricated, and, over time, rod wear that turns a packing job into a rod replacement.

Pulsation is a design and installation issue as much as a wear issue. Gas pulses generated by the piston strokes can excite resonance in the piping if the pulsation dampeners and pipe supports were not sized correctly for the actual operating case, and the result is vibration-induced fatigue cracking in small-bore fittings, not a compressor fault at all. This is one reason pulsation studies are treated as part of API 618 scope rather than an optional extra.

Crosshead and crankcase wear tends to be slower and shows up through oil analysis (particle counts, wear metals) before it shows up as noise or vibration. Lube oil condition on a reciprocating gas compressor deserves the same discipline as it gets on a turbine, covered in our guide to [turbine lube oil analysis](/blog/turbine-lube-oil-analysis/), even though the machine and the failure modes differ.

## Centrifugal Gas Compressor Failure Modes

Surge is the failure mode unique to centrifugal machines and the one operators fear most. It happens when flow through the compressor drops below the point where the impeller can sustain a stable pressure rise, and the gas column in the discharge piping flows backward through the machine before forward flow re-establishes, repeating in a cycle that can run several times a second. A single deep surge event can damage seals, bearings and impeller blades in seconds. Anti-surge control, sized to the actual compressor map and kept in calibration, is not a tuning nicety, it is the primary protection against this failure mode.

Fouling of the gas path, from liquid carryover, wax, salts or compressor oil migrating into the process gas, reduces efficiency gradually and pushes the operating point toward the surge line as head requirement rises against a fixed speed. A compressor that used to run comfortably at a given speed and now needs more speed for the same duty, with head and flow both drifting from the original performance curve, is telling you the gas path is dirty or eroded.

Dry gas seals and, on older machines, oil seals are the other common source of unplanned outages. Seal gas supply quality (dry, filtered, free of liquid dropout) governs seal life more than almost any other single factor, and seal gas system upsets are a frequent root cause behind what looks at first like a seal failure. Our guide to [mechanical seal failure](/blog/mechanical-seal-failure/) covers seal failure mechanisms in more general terms that apply here too, even though dry gas seals on a centrifugal compressor are a specialised variant of the same principle.

Bearing failure on a centrifugal compressor usually shows first in vibration data rather than temperature, because these are typically fluid-film bearings running at high speed, and vibration trending catches the change well before a thermocouple does.

## Symptom, Likely Cause and What to Check

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Falling capacity, rising cylinder discharge temperature (reciprocating) | Worn or leaking suction/discharge valve | Valve inspection, cylinder pressure-volume (indicator) card |
| Gas or oil visible at the distance piece vent | Worn packing, rod misalignment or scoring | Packing condition, rod runout, rod surface finish |
| Repeated low-frequency knocking in the piping (reciprocating) | Pulsation resonance in a bottle or line | Pulsation study review, dampener condition, pipe support integrity |
| Compressor hunting, audible cycling, discharge pressure oscillating | Surge, usually from low flow or a dirty gas path | Anti-surge valve response, actual operating point against the compressor map |
| Efficiency drifting down at the same speed setpoint over weeks (centrifugal) | Fouling, erosion or seal gas contamination | Performance trend against baseline curve, seal gas filtration and dew point |
| Rising vibration with stable temperature (centrifugal) | Bearing wear, coupling misalignment, rotor imbalance | Vibration spectrum, coupling alignment records, balance history |

## Monitoring Between Overhauls

Trending beats spot checks for both machine types, because the value is in the rate of change, not any single reading. For reciprocating machines that means tracking cylinder discharge temperatures, indicator card shape where instrumentation allows it, and lube oil condition at a fixed interval rather than only when something looks wrong. For centrifugal machines it means holding a performance baseline (flow, head, speed, efficiency) taken when the machine was known to be clean, and comparing current readings against that baseline rather than against a generic expectation.

Vibration monitoring earns its place on both families, though the useful frequency ranges and fault signatures differ between a reciprocating machine's low-speed mechanical events and a centrifugal machine's higher-speed rotordynamic behaviour. A condition monitoring programme built around continuous or routine vibration data, oil analysis and, on centrifugal machines, performance trending catches most of the failure modes above while they are still cheap to fix. ## Planning the Overhaul, Not Just Reacting to It

A reciprocating compressor overhaul scope typically runs from valve sets and packing through to piston rings, crosshead components and, on a longer cycle, cylinder liner and rod work, driven by running hours, gas quality and the trend data above rather than a fixed date alone. A centrifugal compressor overhaul is usually driven by a scheduled inspection window (often tied to the driver, if it is a gas turbine, or to a process shutdown) and covers seal replacement, bearing inspection, rotor condition assessment and, where fouling or erosion has progressed, a rotor clean or repair.

Spares lead time is the item that most often turns a planned overhaul into an extended outage. Long-lead items on both machine types, seal cartridges, valve sets, bearings and rotor components, need to be identified and ordered against the forecast overhaul date, not against the date the machine actually stops running. Our guide to [turbine spare parts lead time](/blog/turbine-spare-parts-lead-time/) covers the same procurement problem in a turbine context, and the underlying lesson carries over directly to compressor spares.

Any work that involves opening a pressurised gas system, isolating rotating machinery, or breaking into an electrical supply feeding a compressor driver needs qualified personnel working to a lockout/tagout procedure and a permit to work. This is not a step a facility should shortcut to save a shift, on either machine type.

Getting the overhaul scope right the first time, rather than opening the machine and discovering additional work once it is already stripped, is largely a function of how good the condition data going in was. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main difference between a reciprocating and a centrifugal gas compressor?

A reciprocating compressor uses a piston and valves to compress gas in discrete strokes, which suits variable flow and pressure duty. A centrifugal compressor accelerates gas continuously through a rotating impeller, which suits steady, high-flow duty such as gas injection or feeding a gas turbine train.

### What causes compressor surge and why is it dangerous?

Surge happens on centrifugal compressors when flow drops below the level the machine can sustain a stable pressure rise against, causing the gas to flow backward through the compressor in a repeating cycle. A deep surge event can damage seals, bearings and impeller blades within seconds, which is why anti-surge control has to be calibrated to the actual compressor performance curve.

### How often should a gas compressor be overhauled?

There is no single interval that fits every machine. Reciprocating compressors are usually scoped by running hours, gas quality and valve and packing condition data; centrifugal compressors are usually scoped against a scheduled shutdown window and performance trend data against baseline. The OEM manual for the specific machine, read alongside your own condition data, governs the actual timing.

### Can a facility run a gas compressor on condition monitoring alone, without a fixed overhaul schedule?

Condition monitoring (vibration, oil analysis, performance trending) reduces surprises and helps target the overhaul scope accurately, but most operators still plan overhauls around a shutdown window or a running-hours target, using the condition data to refine that scope rather than to remove planning altogether.

### Why does spares lead time matter more for compressors than for simpler equipment?

Compressor-specific items such as seal cartridges, valve sets and rotor components are often built to order rather than held as generic stock, and lead times can run into months. Ordering against the forecast overhaul date, rather than waiting until the machine actually needs the part, is usually the difference between a planned outage and an extended one.
