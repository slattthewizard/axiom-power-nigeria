# Power System Harmonics: What Every Plant Manager Should Know

If a plant has picked up nuisance trips on the main breaker, a transformer running hotter than its load suggests, or a generator whose voltage hunts under load, power system harmonics are a reasonable suspect. Harmonics are distortion in the current and voltage waveform caused by non-linear loads, and on a modern Nigerian factory, hospital or data floor they are almost never absent. The question is not whether harmonics exist on site, it is whether they have reached a level that is doing damage.

This article sets out where harmonics come from, what total harmonic distortion (THD) actually measures, what harmonics do to transformers, capacitor banks and generators, and the practical options for bringing distortion back under control. It is written for plant managers and maintenance managers, not for the electrical theory exam.

## Where Harmonics Come From

Harmonics are generated by any load that draws current in a shape other than a clean sine wave. On an industrial site the usual sources are:

- Variable frequency drives (VFDs) on pumps, fans and compressors, which rectify the incoming AC to DC before inverting it back to a variable-frequency output.
- UPS systems and battery chargers, which also rectify the supply.
- LED lighting drivers and electronic ballasts.
- Arc and resistance welding equipment.
- Switched-mode power supplies in IT and telecoms racks.
- Variable-speed soft starters and some solid-state relays.

None of these loads are unusual or a sign of poor design. A plant with more VFDs and UPS load than it had ten years ago will carry more harmonics than it used to, simply because the electronic front ends on that equipment chop the sine wave into pulses rather than drawing it smoothly. See the sibling guide on [variable frequency drives](/blog/variable-frequency-drive-guide/) for how a VFD's rectifier stage produces this effect.

## What THD Actually Measures

Total harmonic distortion expresses how far a voltage or current waveform departs from a pure 50 Hz sine wave, as a percentage of the fundamental. A clean supply has a THD close to zero. A supply feeding a large VFD load with no mitigation can carry current THD well above 20 per cent at the drive terminals, though the figure that matters for plant health is usually measured further back, at the switchboard or point of common coupling with other loads.

Two figures get used, and mixing them up leads to wrong conclusions:

- Voltage THD (VTHD) describes distortion in the voltage waveform, which affects every load fed from that bus, including sensitive electronics and the generator's own voltage regulator.
- Current total demand distortion (TDD) describes distortion in the current drawn by a specific load, expressed against the maximum demand load current rather than the instantaneous current, which avoids overstating distortion at light load.

A single spot reading is a snapshot, not a diagnosis. Harmonic content changes with which drives and chargers are running, so a proper assessment trends VTHD and current TDD over a working shift or a week, not a five-minute check.

## What Harmonics Do to Plant Equipment

Distorted current and voltage are more than a bad reading on an analyser screen, they cause real thermal and mechanical stress. The table below covers the failure signs most often reported on Nigerian industrial sites and what typically drives them.

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Transformer running hot for its load | Harmonic currents (especially odd triplen harmonics) increase eddy current and stray losses beyond the nameplate rating | Thermal image the transformer at full harmonic load, review the K-factor if the transformer was sized for linear load only |
| Neutral conductor or neutral bar warm to the touch | Triplen harmonics (3rd, 9th, 15th) from single-phase non-linear loads add in the neutral instead of cancelling | Measure neutral current directly, it can exceed phase current on a heavily loaded lighting or IT circuit |
| Power factor correction capacitor bank tripping or failing early | Harmonic currents resonate with the capacitor bank's reactance, amplifying distortion at a particular frequency | Check bank design for detuning reactors; an un-detuned bank on a harmonic-rich bus is a known failure pattern, see the sibling guide on [power factor correction](/blog/power-factor-correction-capacitor-banks/) |
| Breakers or protection relays tripping with no obvious overload | Harmonic currents distort the RMS reading seen by some electromechanical and older electronic relays, or genuinely raise RMS current above the trip setting | Log waveform capture at the next trip rather than resetting and moving on |
| Generator voltage hunting or overshooting under load | The AVR's sensing circuit reacts to a distorted voltage waveform, and non-linear loads with a leading displacement can destabilise excitation control | Review load composition against the [generator sizing guide](/blog/generator-sizing-guide/) and have the AVR and sensing checked by a qualified technician |
| Alternator windings running hotter than expected for the kW load | Harmonic currents add I²R losses that do not show up on a standard ammeter reading true RMS incorrectly, or that a cheap meter misses entirely | Use a true RMS meter or power quality analyser, not a basic clamp meter, when investigating alternator heating |

## Harmonics and Generators Specifically

Generators deserve their own section because a genset alternator is a smaller, higher-impedance source than the grid, so the same non-linear load produces proportionally more voltage distortion on generator power than on grid power. Two effects matter most to a maintenance manager:

The automatic voltage regulator senses the output waveform to hold voltage steady. A heavily distorted waveform, particularly with a leading power factor caused by an over-corrected capacitor bank, can push the AVR into instability, showing up as voltage hunting or overshoot rather than a steady setpoint. The alternator itself also runs hotter than its kW loading alone would predict, because harmonic currents add resistive losses the way any current does, whether the fundamental or a harmonic. On a set that is already running close to its rated output, this can shorten winding life even though the load meter shows nothing unusual.

Where a plant is adding VFD or UPS load onto a genset supply, it is worth reviewing derating with whoever sized the set, alongside the alternator manufacturer's guidance on non-linear load percentage.

## Measuring Harmonics Properly

A basic multimeter or standard clamp meter will not tell a plant manager anything useful about harmonics. The right instrument is a power quality analyser capable of capturing individual harmonic orders, VTHD, current TDD and waveform snapshots over a logging period, connected at the switchboard feeding the loads in question, not just at one drive. A useful investigation logs at least a full production shift, including start-up and shutdown of major loads, since harmonic content changes sharply as drives ramp and UPS units switch between mains and battery. The output an engineer needs is a harmonic spectrum showing which orders (5th, 7th, 11th, 13th and the triplens) dominate, since a single percentage figure alone does not point to the source or the correct fix.

## Mitigation Options

There is no single fix that suits every site, and the right answer depends on where the distortion is coming from and how much of it there is. The options in common use, roughly in order of cost and complexity:

Line reactors, fitted ahead of a VFD or rectifier, add impedance that smooths current draw and reduces harmonic injection with the least cost and complexity. Twelve-pulse or eighteen-pulse rectifier configurations on larger drives cancel out specific harmonic orders by design, at higher equipment cost, and suit new large VFD installations more than a retrofit. Passive harmonic filters, tuned to specific problem frequencies, are a mature and relatively low-cost retrofit but need engineering to avoid creating a new resonance elsewhere on the bus. Active harmonic filters inject a corrective waveform in real time and handle a mixed, changing load profile better than a passive filter, at higher capital cost. Detuned capacitor banks, already recommended for power factor correction on a harmonic-rich bus, avoid resonance with existing distortion rather than adding new filtering. Alternator uprating, or specifying a lower sub-transient reactance alternator on a new generator order, reduces the voltage distortion a given non-linear load produces on that set.

Any of these needs a proper site survey and, ideally, the harmonic spectrum captured above rather than a guess. A [power plant audit](/power-plant-audit-nigeria/) that includes power quality logging is the right starting point before specifying a filter.

## IEEE 519 in General Terms

IEEE 519 is the recommended practice most commonly referenced for harmonic limits at the point where a plant connects to its supply. It sets out target limits for voltage distortion and for current distortion relative to a load's demand, intended to keep one customer's non-linear load from degrading power quality for others sharing the same point of common coupling. It is a recommended practice rather than a Nigerian statutory code, and it is not the only reference in use, but it remains the benchmark that harmonic filter suppliers and consulting engineers design against. Where a site supplies its own captive generation rather than sharing a public feeder, the practical limits that matter are the ones the generator and its AVR can tolerate, alongside whatever the OEM alternator datasheet specifies for non-linear load percentage.

Bringing a site's harmonic profile within a workable range, whether that is judged against IEEE 519 or an OEM's own limit, generally starts with a proper measurement and a scoped mitigation plan rather than buying a filter first and measuring after. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can harmonics damage a generator even if the load never exceeds its kVA rating?

Yes. Harmonic currents add heating in the alternator windings on top of the heating from the fundamental load current, so a set can run within its kVA rating on a standard meter reading and still overheat or show AVR instability if the load is heavily non-linear. The kVA meter alone does not tell the whole story on a distorted waveform.

### Is a standard clamp meter good enough to check for harmonics?

A basic clamp meter reads RMS current reasonably well on most modern meters but tells you nothing about harmonic spectrum or THD, and cheap averaging-type meters can misread a distorted waveform altogether. Diagnosing a suspected harmonics problem needs a true power quality analyser with harmonic capture, not a general-purpose multimeter.

### Does adding a bigger transformer or generator fix a harmonics problem?

Oversizing the source reduces the impedance the harmonic currents see, which can lower voltage distortion somewhat, but it does not remove the harmonic currents themselves and does nothing for capacitor bank stress or neutral loading. It is a partial mitigation at best and usually more expensive than fitting the right filter at the load.

### Do I need a harmonic filter on every VFD in the plant?

Not necessarily. Small individual drives on a large, stiff supply often contribute negligible distortion. Filtering decisions should follow a measured harmonic spectrum at the switchboard rather than a blanket rule, since over-filtering wastes budget and under-filtering leaves the real problem sources untouched.

### What is the difference between THD and TDD?

THD compares harmonic content to the instantaneous fundamental, which can look alarming at light load even when the absolute harmonic current is small. TDD compares harmonic current to the load's maximum demand current, giving a steadier and more representative figure for compliance and mitigation decisions, which is why IEEE 519 uses TDD rather than THD for current limits.
